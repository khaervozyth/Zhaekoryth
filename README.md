# Zhaekoryth

> The bridge crossed the river only when the river forgot its banks.

## An Account of the Hollow Crossing

There are two villages beside the river of Zhaekoryth. One appears on every map. The other appears only in the spaces left between names. Their inhabitants exchange lanterns during the longest night, sending them across a bridge that cannot be seen from either bank.

The lanterns arrive dry, even during floods. Their flames lean toward the village they have left. Some return carrying a different color of fire; others return unlit, with a small silver scale resting where the wick should have been.

No villager admits to having built the bridge. The oldest songs mention its destruction. The oldest stones show signs of having been laid tomorrow.

## The Cartographer's Error

A cartographer attempted to place both villages on a single sheet. He began with the river and worked outward, drawing orchards, fields, and roads. When he finished, both villages occupied the same place, and the bridge formed a circle around them.

He called the drawing an error. The villagers called it an accurate account of the longest night.

The cartographer slept beside the river. Upon waking, he found a circular wet mark beneath his bed. His boots had filled with ash. The map now showed three villages, the third situated inside the margin of the paper.

He erased the third village. Somewhere across the water, a bell rang thirteen times. There were no bells in either village, and the cartographer had used no ink to draw one.

## The Shape Beneath the Current

During the lantern exchange, children sometimes see a long shape moving beneath the bridge. It has a dragon's head and a body that appears to bend around the river rather than follow it. Its scales reflect houses whose doors face inward.

The creature never rises. Its eyes remain open, showing a sky where the lanterns float like stars. When a lantern goes dark above the water, a corresponding star appears within one of those eyes.

The elders forbid counting the stars. A child once counted seven and found eight shadows waiting beside his bed. They followed him through the village until he learned to stop recognizing the eighth as his own.

The child grew old. In his final account, he wrote that the eighth shadow had never followed him. It had been walking ahead, looking for a bank the river had forgotten.

## The Orchard Beyond the Margin

The cartographer's third village reappears whenever the map is folded toward the river. Its houses stand beneath an orchard of leafless trees. Small lights hang from the branches, each shaped like a lantern seen from underwater.

One house has a door painted across its roof. Another has windows filled with the same pale sky visible in the creature's eyes. The final house remains unfinished in every account, although travelers insist they have heard someone moving inside it.

A woman tried to describe the orchard in a letter. Her sentences ended before she reached the house. On the following page, the unfinished sentences appeared as paths, each leading to the roof of a different building.

She sealed the letter. The wax cooled into the shape of a bridge.

## The Words Carried by an Unlit Lantern

**Zhae — kor — yth — ivren.**

These sounds are sung by the lantern bearers, but their positions change according to which bank a singer remembers as home. The final sound is sometimes held for the duration of the crossing. Sometimes it is omitted, and the lantern arrives before the singer has begun.

An elder once proposed a translation. Before he could speak it, the river stopped reflecting the village. He withdrew his proposal, and the reflection returned with one additional house.

The house had no counterpart on the bank. Its door remained open throughout the longest night. At dawn, something inside blew out a lantern, and a small silver scale appeared on the elder's tongue.

## Questions Asked from the Wrong Bank

> Which village receives the first lantern?
>
> The one whose windows are facing away.
>
> Where does the bridge end?
>
> Where the returning flame begins to lean.
>
> Who lives beyond the margin?
>
> Whoever folded the river before the ink was dry.

The questions appear in several songs, though none preserves the same melody. When sung beside the water, the answers can be heard from beneath the current. When sung far inland, they are answered by the sound of footsteps crossing an empty room.

The lantern bearers have stopped asking the final question. They continue leaving a space for its answer.

## The Bank That Remains Unnamed

Every year, the villages prepare another exchange. They polish the lantern glass, gather the silver scales, and avoid looking directly at the place where the bridge ought to be.

The cartographer's map is kept folded. Its margin remains damp in every season. Occasionally, a new path appears among the unfinished sentences, leading toward a house whose roof has finally acquired a door.

Zhaekoryth crosses the river when nobody remembers the crossing. Beneath the water, an open eye waits for a star that has not yet gone dark.
